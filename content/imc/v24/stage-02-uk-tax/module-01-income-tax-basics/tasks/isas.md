An **Individual Savings Account (ISA)** is a tax wrapper. It is not an investment in itself. Whatever you hold inside it grows free of UK income tax and capital gains tax. You do not need to report it on a tax return.

**Annual limit.** For 2026-27 an adult can pay in up to **£20,000** in total across all their ISAs. Unused allowance cannot be carried forward to the next year.

**Types of ISA:**
- **Cash ISA:** savings accounts.
- **Stocks and shares ISA:** funds, shares, bonds and similar investments.
- **Innovative finance ISA:** peer-to-peer loans and some crowdfunding.
- **Lifetime ISA (LISA):** for ages 18 to 39 to open. Up to **£4,000** a year, with a 25% government bonus. It counts towards the £20,000 total.
- **Junior ISA:** for under-18s, with a separate limit of **£9,000**.

**Watch for change.** The government has announced that, from April 2027, most savers under 65 will have a lower cap on the cash part of their ISA allowance. Questions are always tied to a specific tax year.

**Precise definition:** an ISA is a government-approved account in which savings and investments are sheltered from UK income tax and capital gains tax, subject to annual subscription limits.
