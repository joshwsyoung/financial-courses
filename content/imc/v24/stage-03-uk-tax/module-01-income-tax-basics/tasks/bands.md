Income tax is charged in slices. Each slice of your income is taxed at its own rate. Moving into a higher band does **not** raise the tax on the income below it.

For **2026-27**, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, the slices are:

| Slice of taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| First £12,570 (the **personal allowance**) | 0% |
| Next £37,700 (the **basic rate band**) | 20% |
| From £50,270 up to £125,140 | 40% (higher rate) |
| Over £125,140 | 45% (additional rate) |

Two details trip people up.

**The allowance tapers.** Once adjusted net income goes over £100,000, the personal allowance falls by £1 for every £2 above that level. It reaches zero at £125,140.

**Scotland is different.** Scottish taxpayers use their own bands and rates for earned income. The IMC usually works with the rest-of-UK figures, but check the question.

**Precise definition:** the personal allowance is the amount of income an individual can receive each tax year before income tax is due. Rate bands are the slices of taxable income above it, each charged at a set rate.

*Figures are tied to the 2026-27 tax year and are updated every April.*
