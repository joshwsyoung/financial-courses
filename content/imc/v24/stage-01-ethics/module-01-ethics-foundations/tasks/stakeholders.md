A **stakeholder** is anyone affected by what you or your firm does. In investment management that list is longer than it first looks.

- **Clients** rely on you to act in their interests and to tell them the truth about risk and cost.
- **Your employer** relies on you to follow its policies and protect its reputation.
- **Colleagues** rely on you to do your part honestly, so their work is not built on errors.
- **Shareholders** of your firm want it to make a profit, but over the long term, not through shortcuts that lead to fines.
- **The market** relies on fair dealing and accurate information, so prices mean something.
- **Regulators** rely on you to be open with them and to report problems.
- **Society** gains when savings are managed well, because that funds pensions, companies and public services.

These interests sometimes clash. A trade that boosts this quarter's profit might harm clients. Ethical behaviour means noticing these clashes and not quietly favouring whoever is closest or loudest.

In regulated financial services the general principle is clear: **client interests come before your own and your firm's.** You can still earn a fair living, but not at the client's expense.

**Precise definition:** a stakeholder is any person or group who can affect, or be affected by, an organisation's decisions and actions.
