Imagine an adviser who puts a client into a product that pays the adviser a large fee. Every form is filled in. Every disclosure is made. Nothing illegal has happened. But the client would have been better off with a cheaper option, and the adviser knew it.

That gap is the difference between **law** and **ethics**.

**Law** is the minimum standard society will enforce. It is written down, it applies to everyone, and breaking it has penalties.

**Ethics** is about what you *should* do, even when nobody is checking and no rule covers the situation. It asks whether an action is honest, fair and in the interests of the people who rely on you.

In finance the two overlap a lot, but not completely:

- Some actions are **legal but unethical**, like the adviser above.
- Rules always lag behind new products and new tricks, so ethics has to fill the gap.
- Regulators increasingly write ethics *into* the rules. In the UK the FCA asks people to act with integrity, not just to follow procedures.

Why does this matter so much in finance? Clients usually know less than the professional. They cannot easily check the advice they get, and mistakes can take years to show up. That makes them dependent on the professional's honesty.

**Precise definition:** ethics is a set of moral principles that guide behaviour beyond what the law requires. Legal compliance is necessary, but it is not enough to be ethical.
