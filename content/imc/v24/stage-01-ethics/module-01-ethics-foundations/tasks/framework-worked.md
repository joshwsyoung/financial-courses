**Scenario.** Priya is a fund manager. A broker her firm uses invites her on an all-expenses weekend in Monaco "to discuss research". Her firm's gifts policy needs approval for anything above £100. The firm is about to review which brokers it uses.

**Step 1: identify the facts.** The trip is worth thousands of pounds. It is well above the £100 threshold. The broker review is coming up, and Priya has a say in it.

**Step 2: stakeholders and duties.** Priya owes a duty to the fund's investors to choose brokers on cost and quality only. Her firm needs her to follow its policy. The broker wants to keep the business.

**Step 3: options.**
(a) Accept quietly. (b) Accept but tell compliance. (c) Decline, or offer to meet the broker in the office instead.

**Step 4: test each option.**
(a) Breaks the policy, and would look terrible in a headline. **Fails.**
(b) Disclosing it is better, but a trip this lavish, timed just before a review, could still sway her judgement, or look as if it does. Disclosure does not fix a conflict this serious. **Weak.**
(c) No policy breach, and no harm to investors. Easy to explain to anyone. **Passes.**

**Step 5: decide, act and reflect.** Priya declines, tells compliance about the offer so they know the broker's practices, and keeps a record.

**Takeaway:** when a benefit is large and closely tied to a decision you will make, **avoiding** the conflict beats **disclosing** it.
