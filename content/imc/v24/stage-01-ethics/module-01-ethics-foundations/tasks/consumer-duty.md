The **Consumer Duty** came into force in July 2023. It raised the bar for how firms treat **retail customers**. It moved the focus from "did we follow the process?" to "did the customer actually get a good outcome?"

It has three layers.

**1. The Consumer Principle.** Firms must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers.

**2. Three cross-cutting rules.** Firms must:
- act in **good faith** towards retail customers;
- **avoid causing foreseeable harm**;
- **enable and support** retail customers to pursue their financial objectives.

**3. Four outcomes.** Firms must get these right and keep checking them:
- **Products and services:** designed to meet the needs of a clearly identified target market.
- **Price and value:** the price is reasonable compared with the benefits.
- **Consumer understanding:** communications help customers make informed decisions.
- **Consumer support:** support is easy to use. Leaving or switching should be no harder than buying.

Firms must also **monitor** the outcomes customers actually get, and fix problems they find.

**Precise definition:** the Consumer Duty is a set of FCA rules (Principle 12 and PRIN 2A). They require firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers through the cross-cutting rules and the four outcomes.
