Think about a babysitter.

You hand over the most important thing you have, and then you leave. You cannot watch what happens. You choose a babysitter because you **trust** them, and maybe because a friend vouched for them.

Now imagine you hear that a babysitter in your town ignored a child to scroll on their phone. Suddenly *every* babysitter looks a bit riskier. Parents ask more questions, pay less, or stay at home.

Finance works the same way. A client hands over their savings and cannot see what happens next. If one firm behaves badly, trust in the whole industry drops. People save less, invest less, and demand more rules.

So trust is not a nice extra in finance. **Trust is the product.** Without it, nobody hands over their money in the first place.

That is why one person's bad behaviour is everyone's problem, and why the industry cares so much about ethics.
