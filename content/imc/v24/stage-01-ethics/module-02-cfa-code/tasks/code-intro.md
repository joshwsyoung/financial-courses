Most professions have a rulebook. For investment professionals, one of the best known comes from CFA Institute, the global body that runs the CFA Program. It has two parts that work together.

The **Code of Ethics** is short. It sets out broad principles: the kind of professional you should be. The **Standards of Professional Conduct** are longer and more specific. They turn the principles into duties you can check your behaviour against.

The Code has six components. In plain terms, they ask you to:

1. be honest, competent, diligent and respectful, and behave ethically with everyone you deal with
2. put the integrity of the profession and your clients' interests ahead of your own
3. take reasonable care and use independent professional judgement
4. behave professionally yourself, and encourage others to do the same
5. support capital markets that are fair, efficient and trusted
6. keep your professional competence up to date, and help others improve theirs

The Code and Standards bind CFA Institute members and CFA Program candidates. Breaches can lead to sanctions, up to losing the right to use the CFA designation. Many UK firms also treat the Standards as a benchmark for good conduct. The IMC expects you to know what they contain and to apply them.

One rule of thumb matters a lot. If the law and the Standards say different things, follow whichever is **stricter**. Doing only what local law allows is not enough when the Standards ask for more.

**Precise definition:** the CFA Institute Code of Ethics is a set of six high-level ethical principles. The Standards of Professional Conduct are seven groups of more specific duties that put those principles into practice.
