Standards II and III are where many dilemmas sit. They protect two groups: the market as a whole, and the clients you serve.

**Standard II** keeps markets fair. Some information is *material*, meaning it would probably move the price. Some is *non-public*, meaning it has not been released to the market. If you hold information that is both, you must not trade on it or pass it on. You may still piece together public facts and minor private details to reach a view. This is called the **mosaic** approach. You must also never manipulate the market, for example by spreading false rumours or trading to give a false picture of demand.

**Standard III** sets out what clients are owed:

- **Loyalty, prudence and care:** act for the client's benefit, putting their interests before your firm's and your own.
- **Fair dealing:** treat all clients fairly when you issue research or allocate trades. Fairly does not mean identically. A premium service is fine if it is disclosed and does not harm other clients.
- **Suitability:** understand each client's objectives, constraints and attitude to risk before you act. Review this regularly.
- **Performance presentation:** make performance figures fair, accurate and complete. Do not pick out only your best accounts.
- **Confidentiality:** keep client information private. The exceptions are when the law requires disclosure, when the client agrees, or when it concerns illegal activity.

**Precise definition:** material non-public information is information that would probably affect a security's price, or that a reasonable investor would want before deciding, and that has not yet been made available to the market generally.
