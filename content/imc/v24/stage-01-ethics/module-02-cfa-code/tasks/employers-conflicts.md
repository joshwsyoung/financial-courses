The last four Standards cover your employer, your work, your conflicts and your link to CFA Institute.

**Standard IV: duties to employers.** Work in your employer's interests. When you leave, do not take its clients, files or trade secrets. If you take on paid outside work that could compete with your job, get written consent from your employer first. If you manage people, take reasonable steps to make sure they follow the rules.

**Standard V: investment analysis, recommendations and actions.** Do the homework. Every recommendation needs a reasonable and adequate basis, backed by research you can explain. Tell clients how you invest and what the main risks and limits are. Make clear what is fact and what is opinion. Keep records that support your work.

**Standard VI: conflicts of interest.** Disclose anything that could reasonably affect your independence and objectivity, in plain language. When you trade, client and employer transactions come before your personal ones. If you receive or pay a fee for referring business, tell the people affected before they sign up.

**Standard VII: responsibilities as a member or candidate.** Do not cheat in CFA Institute exams or share what was in them. When you describe the CFA designation or your candidacy, be accurate and do not exaggerate what it means.

Notice the pattern. Most of these duties come down to three habits. Be open. Put clients first. Be able to show your working.

**Precise definition:** a conflict of interest arises when your own interests, or someone else's, could reasonably be expected to get in the way of your duty to act in the best interests of a client or employer.
