The Standards are long, but they rest on a handful of values. If you understand the values, you can often work out the right answer even when no rule fits exactly.

Towards the people you serve:

- **Integrity:** be honest, even when it costs you.
- **Clients first:** your clients' interests come before your firm's and your own.
- **Fair dealing:** treat every client fairly, not just the biggest or the loudest.

Towards the profession and the market:

- **Independence and objectivity:** let evidence drive your judgement, not pressure or perks.
- **Competence and diligence:** know your subject, keep learning and do the work properly.
- **Market integrity:** protect fair, open markets that everyone can trust.

You can see these values, or their absence, in everyday behaviour.

**Positive indicators** include admitting mistakes quickly and asking compliance when unsure. Recording why advice was given is another. So is turning down a gift that feels too generous.

**Negative indicators** include "everyone does it" excuses and pressure to hit sales targets at any cost. Hiding information from clients or colleagues is another warning sign. So is treating a rule as a box to tick rather than a duty to meet.

A quick test helps. Would you be happy to explain your decision to the client, your manager and the regulator? If not, a value is probably being bent.

**Precise definition:** behavioural indicators are actions or attitudes you can observe. Positive indicators suggest a person or firm is acting in line with ethical values. Negative indicators suggest they are drifting away from them.
